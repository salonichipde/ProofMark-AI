Section 01

Problem Understanding

What is happening

Marketing and brand teams publish content across social media, advertisements, press releases, email campaigns, and websites. Under deadline pressure or as teams grow, published content sometimes:

  • States product specifications that contradict approved internal facts (e.g., wrong battery life, wrong pricing tier)
  • Uses tone, wording, or terminology that violates the brand's own published guidelines
  • Makes broad, unverifiable claims — such as "world's best" or "most trusted" — with no approved evidence behind them
  • Relies on brand guidelines that exist as scattered PDFs or internal wikis that individuals selectively recall

Why it matters

Observation Label
Manual content review requires a reviewer to read and cross-check documents by hand, creating a bottleneck before publication ✅ Verified Fact
Brand guidelines are frequently stored in long, distributed documents rather than structured, queryable formats ✅ Verified Fact
Factual inaccuracies in marketing copy — particularly around product specifications — can undermine customer trust 🔶 Assumption
Small marketing teams and agencies often lack dedicated compliance or brand governance staff 🔶 Assumption

The core gap

There is no lightweight, accessible tool that automatically checks whether a marketing claim is supported by, conflicting with, or simply not addressed by a company's own approved facts and guidelines — and then surfaces that finding clearly to a human reviewer before the content goes live.

Section 03

Proposed Solution

ProofMark AI is an evidence-first brand content governance tool that checks marketing copy against a company's own approved facts and guidelines before it is published.

What ProofMark AI does 💡 Proposed Features

  1. Stores approved brand facts and rules — product specifications, tone guidelines, preferred and banned terminology, and approved claims
  2. Accepts submitted marketing text — a caption, ad copy, press release snippet, or any draft content
  3. Checks each claim in the submitted text against the stored fact base and classifies it:
    • ✅ Supported — claim matches an approved fact
    • ❌ Conflicting — claim directly contradicts an approved fact
    • ⚠️ Insufficient Evidence — claim cannot be verified against any stored fact (flagged as unverifiable, not automatically false)
    • 🔍 Guideline Gap — no relevant guideline or fact exists in the system yet (flagged for the brand manager to fill, not auto-rejected)
  4. Explains every flag — shows which specific fact or rule triggered the classification
  5. Enables human review — a reviewer can comment, request changes, or approve the content
  6. Logs decisions — records what was approved, by whom, and when

What ProofMark AI does NOT do

  • Does not automatically reject or publish content — a human makes every final call
  • Does not invent evidence or search the internet for supporting facts
  • Does not treat a missing guideline as proof that a claim is false
  • Does not replace human judgment — it structures and speeds up reviewer work

Enterprise tools (e.g., Bynder) Brand asset management and workflows Likely expensive and inaccessible to small teams 🔶 Assumption

What makes ProofMark AI different

  • Evidence-first logic: Every flag includes a reason and a specific source fact — no mystery rejections
  • Honest uncertainty: The tool distinguishes "this contradicts an approved fact" from "we simply have no fact on file" — missing evidence is never treated as proof of wrongness
  • Guideline Gap Detector: Instead of silently failing or auto-rejecting, the tool flags that a brand guideline is missing and turns it into an action item for the brand manager
  • Human-in-the-loop by design: The tool assists and structures review; it never replaces the reviewer
  • Accessible scope: Built for small teams, not enterprise procurement budgets

How the AI check works (simplified)

  1. All approved brand facts are loaded as context text
  2. The submitted draft + loaded facts are sent to the LLM with a structured prompt
  3. The prompt asks: "For each claim in this draft, classify it as Supported, Conflicting, Insufficient Evidence, or Guideline Gap — based only on the facts provided. Return structured JSON."
  4. The JSON response is parsed and displayed on the frontend with colour-coded annotations and explanations
Known risk: LLM outputs can vary in format or reasoning. Mitigation: Enforce strict JSON output format in the prompt and validate the structure before displaying results.

Section 10

Expected Impact

Direct impact

  • Reduces time spent on manual brand fact-checking before publication
  • Reduces the likelihood of publishing product claims that contradict approved specifications
  • Creates a traceable, timestamped record of what was reviewed and approved
  • Turns missing guidelines into explicit action items rather than silent failures

Who benefits

  • Copywriters who want fast, structured feedback before a draft goes to human review
  • Brand managers who want to stop correcting the same factual errors repeatedly
  • Reviewers and legal teams who need a documented approval audit trail
Scale potential 🔶 Assumption
Small marketing agencies and in-house brand teams that lack dedicated compliance staff are the most likely early adopters. The evidence-first and human-in-the-loop governance model could extend to regulated sectors where marketing claims carry legal weight — such as healthcare, financial services, and food and nutrition.

Section 12

Demo Scenario — NovaSound (fictional company)

NovaSound is a fictional consumer audio brand created for this demonstration. All facts, figures, and copy below are invented for illustrative purposes only.

Setup

NovaSound makes wireless headphones. Their marketing team uses ProofMark AI to check campaign copy before it goes live. The brand manager has loaded the following approved facts into the system:

Fact ID Approved Fact
NS-01 NovaSound headphones offer up to 10 hours of battery life.
NS-02 NovaSound is designed for commuters and remote workers.
NS-03 NovaSound uses 40 mm custom-tuned drivers.

Note: No approved fact exists yet regarding market rankings, trust ratings, or awards.

Draft marketing copy submitted for review

"NovaSound headphones deliver up to 15 hours of battery life, making them India's most trusted wireless headphones for everyday use."

ProofMark AI output

Claim Classification Explanation
"up to 15 hours of battery life" ❌ Conflicting Approved fact NS-01 states "up to 10 hours." The draft overstates the specification by 5 hours.
"India's most trusted wireless headphones" ⚠️ Insufficient Evidence No approved fact supports a market ranking or trust rating. This claim is flagged as unverifiable against stored facts. It is not automatically labelled false — it cannot be approved without supporting evidence being added to the fact store.
"for everyday use" ✅ Supported Consistent with approved fact NS-02 (designed for commuters and remote workers).

Reviewer action

The human reviewer reads the flagged results and adds a comment:

"Two issues require attention: correct the battery claim to match NS-01 (10 hours), and either remove the trust ranking claim or provide an approved, sourced fact before this goes live."

Status set to Changes Requested. Draft returned to the copywriter with the comment attached.

Why this scenario works for judging

  • Three out of four classification types are demonstrated in a single, realistic piece of copy
  • It shows that the tool does not auto-reject "India's most trusted" — it flags it honestly as unverifiable
  • It shows a human reviewer staying in control of the final decision
  • The scenario is simple enough to build and demonstrate live within a hackathon timeframe
